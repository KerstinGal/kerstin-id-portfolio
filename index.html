About

Ten years of making complex topics learnable.

I'm a senior instructional designer with over ten years in L&D, mostly inside complex, regulated organisations — manufacturing, tech, and professional services, including post-merger environments and full LMS rollouts.

My background pairs academic grounding — a Magister in German as a Foreign Language, an MBA with distinction, and certification as a TestDaF examiner — with the practical side of the job: storyboarding, building in Storyline and Rise, packaging for the LMS, and keeping stakeholders aligned from brief to launch.

Is it clear what should change, can we tell afterwards whether it did, and did I make something people would rather open than avoid? Two out of three is a passing grade. Three out of three is the job done right.

Built Backward From the Decision

A 5-module programme design, not a delivered client project

Most AI-literacy training explains what the tool does. This curriculum is built backward from the moment that actually matters — when someone has to decide whether to trust an AI-drafted output under real time pressure.

The catch

How I work

From brief to behaviour change.

A structured approach that doesn't fall apart under deadline pressure.

01

Frame the problem

What should be different afterwards, and how will we know? "People should know about X" is a topic, not an objective.

Deliverable · rough concept
02

Design the decisions

Objectives, structure, and the moments that actually matter — usually the calls learners get wrong under pressure.

Deliverable · storyboard
03

Build

Prototype, then full build in Storyline or Rise, reviewed in Articulate Review so stakeholders react to the real thing.

Deliverable · working module
04

Ship properly

SCORM/xAPI packaging, LMS upload, testing, and the slightly awkward learner comms explaining why this training exists.

05

Close the loop

Measurement tied back to the objectives: did behaviour change? Completion tells you they finished — not that it worked.
