Curriculum Design · AI Literacy & Compliance

Built Backward From the Decision

A 5-module programme design, not a delivered client project

Most AI-literacy training explains what the tool does. This curriculum is built backward from the moment that actually matters — when someone has to decide whether to trust an AI-drafted output under real time pressure. Five modules, each anchored to a decision rather than a topic.

Why it's not a checklist

A completion badge proves someone clicked through. It doesn't prove judgment. So the assessment lives inside the learning environment itself — scenario-based, automatically scored — rather than relying on managers reviewing real work, which doesn't scale and raises its own compliance questions in Germany.

Storyline 360 · Compliance

The Close

Gifts & hospitality in the grey zone

A scenario-based compliance micro-module. The learner faces a client invitation during a live negotiation, where two of the three options sound perfectly reasonable. The teaching point is the trap: the risk isn't the value of the gift or whether it's pre-approved — it's the timing. Accepting hospitality while a decision is still open looks like it's for the decision, and "I got it approved" doesn't fix that.

Why it looks so quiet

The design is deliberately restrained, almost text-only. Compliance sells trust, and restraint reads as trust. And there's no gamification, on purpose — points and badges would cheapen the one thing the module needs the learner to feel: that a bad call has real, delayed consequences. The game is the decision, not a scoreboard.

Fortune Teller

A schoolyard game, repurposed for L&D

The paper fortune teller everyone folded as a kid — pick a colour, pick a number, lift the flap — rebuilt in Storyline to deliver instructional-design insights. Behind each number is a short, sharp, usable idea about how learning works and where it usually goes wrong.

Why the opposite of The Close

I don't have one house style — I choose the register that fits the content. This one is playful, colourful and quick, to show that "fun" and "professional" aren't a trade-off — and to stretch the Storyline craft (custom states, layered branching) that a restrained module never puts on display.

How I work

From brief to behaviour change.

A structured approach that doesn't fall apart under deadline pressure. Each phase ends in something a stakeholder can see and sign off.

01

Frame the problem

Before any content: what should be different afterwards, and how will we know? If the answer is "people should know about X," that's a topic, not an objective — so we fix that first.

Deliverable · rough concept, signed off
02

Design the decisions

Objectives, structure, and the moments that actually matter — usually the calls learners get wrong under pressure. I design for those, not for the quiz.

Deliverable · storyboard
03

Build

Prototype, then full build in Storyline or Rise, reviewed in Articulate Review so stakeholders react to the real thing, not a spec.

Deliverable · working module
04

Ship properly

SCORM/xAPI packaging, LMS upload, testing, learner comms. I handle the handover, not just the handoff.

05

Close the loop

Measurement tied back to the objectives: did behaviour change? Completion tells you they finished — it doesn't tell you it worked.
